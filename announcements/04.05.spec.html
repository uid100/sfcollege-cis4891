Project Specifications

I've genuinely enjoyed reading your project proposals. It seems that many of you are excited about the projects you are proposing. I am looking forward to seeing how they develop and evolveover the course of the semester.

Between your presentations and the email that I have received, I know that although many of you are eager to get started, and that some of you may not have a complete picture of the final project in mind. That's okay. In fact, it's most often the case. Change happens. New ideas emerge. Problems are discovered. Opportunities and solutions present themselves.

The traditional software development life cycle (SDLC) is often depicted as a linear sequence of steps. In reality, it's much more iterative and cyclical. In a perfect world, the project specifications would be fully defined before any work begins. In reality, the specifications evolve as the project progresses.

The primary focus of this course is not on the final product, but on the process of building it. The fact that you are building something is important, but the journey you take to get there is what is going to walk you through the essential skills and experiences that you will meet as a software developer.

In my last announcement, I shared with you the reality of having many projects in various stages of development at any given time. Let's revisit that idea.

The current objective of these early stages, investigation, research, concept development and specification, is to build a good starting point. From this, you should have enough information to describe the few top-level requirements of a software project.

Many projects start with a simple idea that is refined over time. As you begin to build your projects, you will likely discover new requirements, constraints, and opportunities that will shape the final product. This is a natural part of the development process.

In the next steps, you will be looking at data components and formalizing requirements. This will help you to further define the scope and functionality of your project. Remember, the goal is to create a solid foundation that you can build upon as you progress through the development process.

 

Don't get too caught up in trying to define everything perfectly at this stage. The key is to have a clear enough understanding of your project's goals and requirements to get started. Don't get so lost in details that it prevents you from making progress. Embrace the iterative nature of software development and the value of artifacts that evolve over time.
