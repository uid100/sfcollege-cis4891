Parallel Development Process and Shared Experiences

This course is specifically designed to give you some flexibility in your projects within some specific parameters. The overall scope is intended to be broad enough to allow for a wide variety of projects, but specific enough to ensure that we experience similar challenges and development phases and typical software development processes and best-practices and common artifacts.

In spite of the variety of projects, the shared experiences and common processes and artifacts still provide an opportunity for collaborative learning and peer support. For example, large projects are often generalized by the different review phases they undergo throughout the development process. Each of these reviews in a formal development cycle has a well-defined set of entry- and exit-criteria. These criteria are intended to ensure that the project is ready to move on to the next phase of development.

Preparing the artifacts for these reviews is an important part of the development process. They serve as a way to communicate the current state of the project and progress and to identify any issues or challenges that need to be addressed. They also provide an opportunity for feedback and collaboration with peers and stakeholders.

A student capstone project is sometimes structured as an independent project developed in isolation, following a defined set of criteria. My hope is that through the shared experiences and collaborative feedback, we learn from each other. I wonder how many of you have seen what someone else is doing and decided to implement something similar in your own project. This is a common occurrence in software development. Software applications evolve over time and benefit from the contributions of other projects and developers.

The discussion projects in this course are designed to let you exercise your creativity and develop particular entry-criteria and artifacts, as well as to see what others are doing, and to give you the very real experience of having your work benefit from being peer-reviewed by others. Please don't underestimate the value of these assignments. Focus on their objective and you'll get more out of them.

 

As you continue to work on your projects, don't be reluctant to learn and borrow ideas from others. Leverage the shared experiences and collaborative feedback to improve your own project. It may feel like you're a team of one, but you are part of a community of others going through similar experiences. Help each other, and learn from each other.

Don't forget, the Discord channel is a good place to ask questions and share insights. We may not be sitting in class together, but that doesn't keep us from discussing projects and progress.
